# Statistical Analysis Findings

## ANOVA: Savings Rate Across Countries

A one-way ANOVA test found statistically significant differences in savings rates across countries.
The reported p-value was below 0.05.
This supports the conclusion that country or region is meaningfully associated with household savings behaviour.

## Pearson Correlation: Family Size and Income Per Capita

Pearson correlation analysis found that larger families are associated with lower income per capita.
This represents a negative relationship between family size and income available per household member.

## Mann-Whitney U Test: Single vs Multi-Earner Households

A Mann-Whitney U test identified differences in relative spending rates between single-earner and multi-earner households.
Multi-earner households generally showed lower relative spending rates.

## OLS Regression: Monthly Savings

An OLS regression model explained approximately 89.9% of the variation in monthly savings.
Important drivers included:
- Household income
- Family size
- Expense ratio
Family size and expense ratio acted as negative drivers of monthly savings.

## Monthly Savings Regression

Monthly savings could be predicted with very high accuracy, with R² close to 1.00.
This is because monthly savings is close to a deterministic function of:
- Income
- Taxes
- Expenses

## Number of Earners and Income Per Capita

Households with more earners tend to have higher income per capita.
However, this relationship weakens for very large families.
