# Household Financial Clusters

K-Means clustering identified seven distinct household financial profiles.
The number of clusters was selected using the elbow method and silhouette score.
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was used to visualise the clusters.

## Cluster 0 — Low-Income Surviving Households

Characteristics:
- Low household income
- Limited savings capacity
- High expense ratio relative to income
These households have limited financial flexibility.

## Cluster 1 — Affluent Mid-Savers

Characteristics:
- Solid household income
- Healthy savings behaviour
- Consistent savings patterns

## Cluster 2 — High-Income Stretched Spenders

Characteristics:
- High household income
- High monthly expenses
- Reduced savings potential despite strong income

## Cluster 3 — Financially Distressed Households

This is the most financially vulnerable segment.
Characteristics:
- Low savings rate
- High expense ratio
- Weak financial resilience

## Cluster 4 — Low-Income Large Families

Characteristics:
- Larger family size
- Relatively low household income
- Low income per capita

## Cluster 5 — High-Income Low-Efficiency Savers

Characteristics:
- High household income
- Low savings efficiency relative to earning potential
These households earn strongly but do not convert income into savings efficiently.

## Cluster 6 — Emerging Market Middle Households

Characteristics:
- Middle-income households
- Greater representation from emerging-market regions
- Moderate savings behaviour

## Financial Vulnerability Across Clusters

A meaningful share of households belongs to financially vulnerable clusters characterised by:
- Low savings rates
- High expense ratios
- Lower financial resilience

## Country and Family Representation

Some countries and larger family structures are over-represented in financially distressed clusters.
This pattern was identified through the country-cluster heatmap.
